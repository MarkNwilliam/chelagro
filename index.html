The problem

Fungal disease is eating export income — and so is rejection.

Anthracnose, late blight and black pod ruin harvests. Then EU/UK border checks reject what survives for pesticide residue (MRL) breaches. Farmers in certified organic schemes need approved inputs, but imported copper costs a premium and ~55% of cheaper local agrochemicals are fake or adulterated.

Lost harvests

Fungal disease destroys 20–40% of susceptible export crops before they reach the market.

Border rejections

MRL breaches get whole containers rejected — a direct loss for exporters and outgrowers.

Fake chemicals

An estimated 55% of agrochemicals in Uganda are counterfeit or sub-standard. Farmers spray and lose anyway.

Organic gap

Certified organic growers have almost no affordable, approved crop protection available locally.

Our solution

Chelated copper. Made here. Low dose. MRL-clean.

Copper diglycinate chelates the copper so far less is needed to control disease — and far less residue remains. That keeps export crops inside EU MRL limits and inside the organic-input rules, at a price local farmers can afford.

  • Organic-approved: copper is an EU Annex II / NOP-listed organic input, and the 4–6 kg Cu/ha/yr cap favours a low-dose chelate.
  • MRL-safe: low residue keeps harvests acceptable to EU and UK regulators.
  • Affordable: 100 g sachets at UGX 13,000–15,000 — undercutting imported copper.
  • Locally made: import substitution, fresher stock and a ~20–30% lower shelf price.
  • Farmers trained: simple dosing instructions in local languages and free field training.
